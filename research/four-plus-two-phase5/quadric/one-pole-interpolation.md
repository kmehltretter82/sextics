# A fixed parameter point can compute a full moving trace

Let $C$ be a smooth projective integral curve in characteristic zero, and $p$ a smooth rational point after any necessary constant extension. Extend scalars to the rational function field $K$ of the physical variables, **including the free flux variable $t$**. Let $R\in K(C)^\times$ be a moving rational function with some simple zeros $D$ at which a desired function $f$ is defined. Work generically so $R(p)\ne0,\infty$. For any specified $f\in\Gamma(D,\mathcal O_D)$, interpolate a function $g\in K(C)$ with these properties:

- Its only possible pole is at $p$.
- It agrees with $f$ along $D$.
- It vanishes at every other zero of $R$ and at every pole of $R$.

All evaluation requirements concern a finite scheme disjoint from $p$. Riemann–Roch or Serre vanishing applied to $\mathcal O_C(np)$ proves surjectivity of its restriction to that scheme for large $n$. Hence such an interpolant exists over $K$. Extra jet conditions or vanishing factors may be imposed on disjoint finite sets in exactly the same way. Since a logarithmic derivative on a curve has only simple poles, the residue theorem gives the **unnormalized full trace**

$$\operatorname{Tr}_{D/K}f=-\operatorname{res}_p\left(g\,\frac{d_C R}{R}\right).$$

Here $d_C$ treats the physical variables as constants. Agreement on each simple zero gives its ordinary field trace including residue degrees. A zero occurring with multiplicity contributes its multiplicity if the reduced value is interpolated. Extra zeros or poles contribute zero. The same interpolation with $f=1$ calculates the unnormalized degree. Coefficients of $g$ are allowed to depend rationally on $t$: their denominators can and do carry the moving polar support. There is no claim that a fixed, parameter-independent jet of the raw current recovers its full trace.

## Contact-chart application

For a curve of smooth marked contacts, set $q=U+V\alpha^2$. The cubic $h$ becomes $A+\alpha B$ after setting $\alpha^2=-U/V$. On $VB\ne0$ its common root is $\alpha=-A/B$, and the moving resultant is $R=UB^2+VA^2$, up to clearing parameter denominators. On the generically étale, one-root locus the corresponding zeros of $R$ on the compact parameter curve give exactly the sheets of the moving ratio fiber. Actual sign, degree and normalization choices must be retained if multiple curves are combined. A chart-denominator factor should be cleared and classified; an extraneous zero is not a sheet of the intended trace. A generic ratio pair avoids fixed exceptional parameter values of the curve.

Each of the three raw current coordinates is a rational function on this moving divisor, now over $K=k(r,s,t)$. In the chart one can represent it using $\alpha=-A/B$, $\beta=-Q_1(1,r,\alpha,s\alpha)$, the given tangent/residue normalization, and its actual $t^2-\alpha^2$ denominator. Interpolating **that function** and applying the one-point residue formula computes its entire trace. This differs from localizing only the three polynomial coefficients of its divergence. Apply differentiation in the physical variables after recovering the rational trace, or differentiate the fixed-uniformizer residue together with its interpolant. Choosing an input with the required compact pairings and evaluating a rational correction remain separate steps. The interpolation on the full coupled parameter curve has not yet been carried out.

## Projective implementation and complementary zeros

Take a projective model of the chosen curve and a known affine chart containing $p$. After a generic field extension if needed, take hyperplanes $H,L$ through $p$ with $H$ transverse there. Require $H$ to meet the curve away from its boundary and singularities, and $L$ to avoid all the other points of that section; such pairs exist over an infinite field. Pick $L_0$ nonzero at these points. In the affine chart write $h_0=H/Y_0$, $l=L/Y_0$. A function $P/H^n$ with $P$ homogeneous of degree $n$ cannot have poles outside $H=0$. The congruence

$$l^nP_{\rm aff}=0\pmod{I_C+(h_0^n)}$$

imposes regularity at every point over $H=0$ except $p$. Indeed $l$ is a unit at the other points, while at $p$ its vanishing order is at least that of $h_0$. Interpolating $f$ and imposing zero values at the complementary divisors are further linear congruences. For example, if the good divisor is cut on a chart by $R_N=0$ and $f=f_N/f_D$ there with $f_D$ a unit, impose $f_D P_{\rm aff}-h_0^n f_N\equiv0\pmod{(I_C,R_N)}$ after saturating away unwanted components. Known factors in the homogenized numerator can enforce vanishing at infinity or chart poles, as long as they are units on the good divisor. At a fixed $n$ and fixed bounds on the auxiliary multipliers, a polynomial ideal identity is a linear system over $K$. The moving $t$ dependence occurs only in its coefficients.

For sufficiently high $n$ the projective numerator ansatz realizes the Riemann–Roch interpolation: on a smooth model multiply a function regular off $p$ by $H^n$ and extend its resulting section using Serre vanishing for the projective ideal. On a nonnormal model impose extra zeros to the conductor at its finitely many singular points, so the function descends there. One may require additional vanishing at a bad point instead of a prescribed nonzero value there only if it is not a desired sheet. A candidate identity on a specified component through a smooth point can be verified in the local ideal by multiplication with a polynomial that is a unit at the point. The rank-open makes the full affine contact scheme locally a regular curve; checking a full ideal identity on every component would unnecessarily impose interpolation data on other components. Computing the ideals, the interpolation spaces and the pole order can still be costly. The method computes a linear trace functional and does not require listing all its geometric points.

For an actual global parameter differential, use $(P/H^n)d(L_0/H)$ instead. The same congruence with $l^{n+2}$ and $h_0^{n+2}$ cancels the double pole from $d(L_0/H)$ at all other hyperplane points. Fixed complementary zero divisors may be included in $P$. To see why high degree suffices for representatives of a given second-kind cohomology class, subtract $d\phi$ with $\phi$ matching finite local integrals at all poles and at all requested zeros, allowing its additional pole at $p$. Additional vanishing at the zero divisor of $d(L_0/H)$ and the conductor makes the quotient by this differential a regular function away from the allowed hyperplane points. Homogenization and the previous interpolation then apply. This enforces the actual global pole conditions; matching 96 compact local pairings alone does not do so.

### An explicit selector in the actual 70-equation parameter presentation

In the quadratic 90-coefficient presentation let $y=y_0+u y_1+E z$, where $E$ inserts the 70 checked pivot coordinates. Write $H=u+Lz$ for a chosen row $L$. The derivative $J$ of the quadratic coefficient map $F$ is linear in $y$. Set $M=J((y+y_0)/2)(E-y_1L)$ and $k=J((y+y_0)/2)y_1$. Quadratic polarization gives $Mz+Hk=F(y)-F(y_0)$. On the parameter curve this is zero, so $\det(M)\,z=-H\operatorname{adj}(M)k$. At $p$ the determinant is 8 modulo 11. On any other affine point of the $H=0$ section, at least one $z_j$ is a unit and $\det(M)/H$ is regular. The same property holds even at a ramified complementary point. Direct checks evaluate the midpoint identity on twelve unrelated coefficient vectors and then check all 70 equations on 32 actual formal orders. The two-generic-hyperplane version avoids taking high powers of this determinant. [Parameter checker](parameter_selector.py)

## Complete moving logarithmic-current test on the coupled contact curve

This computation uses the **fixed genus-four physical contact curve**, not the unknown global curve parametrizing all contacts. Over $\mathbf F_{11}$ take the smooth contact defined by the known $Q_2$ and $h$ in physical $\mathbf P^3$, with seed $p=(1,4,6,1)$. On $a=1$ write its coordinates as $b,c,d$. The hyperplane $H=b-4$ has six simple points, none at infinity. The five other points are represented together by the finite algebra with polynomial $c^5-c^4+c^3-2c^2+c+2$ and a linear expression for $d$. Exact lifting in this algebra gives the complementary jets. The first possible nonconstant function with only a pole at $p$ has pole order five. The checker constructs a numerator over $(b-4)^5$, verifies its order at all five other points and its nonzero numerator at $p$, and excludes orders 1 through 4. Since the complete intersection is projectively normal, the homogeneous numerator spaces used in this test realize every possible regular-off-$p$ function of the indicated pole bound. [One-pole function](one_pole_example.py)

Let $\lambda,t$ now be **independent indeterminates**. On the moving divisor $D_\lambda=(b=\lambda)$ compute both coefficients of the logarithmic form $d\log(t-c)$. The interpolants use the seven-dimensional space of functions with pole at most 16 at $p$ and with a zero at every point at infinity. It is obtained by requiring affine numerator degree at most 15 over $(b-4)^{16}$ and vanishing to order 16 at the other five points of the fixed hyperplane. The exact row calculation uses 87 ambient monomials. The coordinate ring of the physical curve is free over $\mathbf F_{11}[b]$ in the basis $1,c,c^2,c^3,d,cd$, as certified by its Gröbner basis. For six independent interpolating functions let $V$ be the columns evaluating their numerators in that basis at $b=\lambda$; let $W$ instead evaluate $c$ times those numerators. The $dt$ condition uses $M=tV-W$. If $P_j(\tau)$ is the numerator jet at $p$ with $\tau=b-4$, the negative seed residue of $P_j/(b-4)^{16}$ against $db/(b-\lambda)$ is $(\lambda-4)^{-16}\sum_{k=0}^{15}[\tau^k]P_j(\tau)(\lambda-4)^k$. This is computed solely at the fixed point.

Writing $F(\lambda,t)=\operatorname{Res}_d(Q_2(1,\lambda,t,d),h(1,\lambda,t,d))$, the resulting bordered determinant verifies the exact polynomial identities $\det M=(\det V)F$ and $\det M_{\rm bord}=(\det V)F_t$. The interpolation factor $\det V$ has degree 80 in $\lambda$. Thus the $dt$ coefficient is $F_t/F$: the poles move with $\lambda$ and the full projected sextic $F$ appears. The $d\lambda$ coefficient uses $-dc/d\lambda=F_\lambda(\lambda,c)/F_t(\lambda,c)$ and an analogous bordered determinant. The checks compare the polynomial identities over $\mathbf F_{11}[\lambda,t]$ for both components, then check that their exterior derivative vanishes. The full answer on this auxiliary coupled test is exactly

$$\operatorname{Tr}_{D_\lambda/k(\lambda)} d\log(t-c)=d\log F(\lambda,t).$$

Its polar support is the projected sextic $F=0$ on the affine $(\lambda,t)$ chart. This is a complete field-valued logarithmic current with a moving denominator, not the Phase 4 three-coordinate current for the target $a^3c^3$.
