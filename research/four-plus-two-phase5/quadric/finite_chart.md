# Four-edge interpolation and bounds for the finite moving fiber

## Four-edge interpolation

Choose a cubic factor of the even sextic on each of the four coordinate ruling lines of the split quadric. Keep the choices that agree at all four shared vertices. These factors give the twelve boundary coefficients $h_{ij}$ in the $4$ by $4$ matrix of the candidate cubic. Suppose $h_{00} h_{03} h_{30} h_{33}\ne0$. Writing $f_{ij}=[w^iz^j]f$, the four interior coefficients follow independently from four scalar equations:
$$
h_{11}=(f_{11}/2+h_{01}h_{10})/h_{00},\quad
h_{12}=(h_{02}h_{13}-f_{15}/2)/h_{03},
$$
$$
h_{21}=(h_{20}h_{31}-f_{51}/2)/h_{30},\quad
h_{22}=(f_{55}/2+h_{23}h_{32})/h_{33}.
$$
Then compare the nine coefficients of $f-h\sigma h$ at $(1,3),(2,2),(2,4),(3,1),(3,3),(3,5),(4,2),(4,4),(5,3)$. They suffice because the four boundary cubics have already been verified and all odd coefficients vanish. This variant has no nested coefficient solve. The four_edges.py checker verifies the formulas and all 49 coefficients on both factors of the coupled mod-11 contact.

For a fixed marked split smooth quadric over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the projective image of all cubic norms in $\mathbb P^{24}$ has codimension nine and degree $2^{14}$. The norm map from $\mathbb P^{15}$ is given by basepoint-free quadratics. A generic bidegree-$(3,3)$ factor is geometrically irreducible and differs projectively from its conjugate. Uniqueness of factorization then gives generic degree two. Intersecting the image with fifteen general hyperplanes gives $2\deg(\mathrm{image})=2^{15}$.

## Effect of the quotient at the smooth sample

At $(r,s)=(4,2)$ in characteristic eleven, the moving square $Z$ is three, $L=Q_1(1,r,\sqrt Z,s\sqrt Z)=8$ and $L^3=P(\sqrt Z)=6$. The normalized quadric $\widehat Q_1=Q_1/L$ has coefficients $(6,1,6,10,0)$. Its value is one for both square roots. Over $\mathbb F_{121}$, the nontrivial cube roots of one satisfy $x^2+x+1=0$. The orbit_check.py test constructs all twelve lifts under the cube-root action, the overall sign of $h$, and $(h,\alpha)\mapsto(\sigma h,-\alpha)$. It checks the moving root, the normalized quadric, and the single-sheet current $(9,9,6)$ at every lift. For the cube action the residue scales by $\zeta^{-2}$ because the generator $3Q_1^2$ scales by $\zeta^2$. For the involution the anti-invariant input $a^3c^3$ changes the sign of the residue. Overall change of sign of $h$ changes both $Dh$ and its residue Jacobian by the same factor.

## Uniform upper bound for isolated solutions

Work over an algebraic closure of the characteristic-zero ratio field. Keep the intrinsic chart's five free $Q_1$ coordinates and sixteen physical cubic coordinates. Compactify the projective direction of $Q_2$ to $B=\mathbb P^5$. Let $x$ be its hyperplane class. Multiplication by the universal nonzero $Q_2$ embeds $\mathcal O_B(-1)^{19}$ into the trivial rank-44 space of even sextics. This map has constant fiber rank, including at singular quadrics, by injectivity of multiplication in the polynomial ring. Its quotient $W$ has rank 25, is generated by global sections, and has $c_i(W)=\binom{18+i}{i}x^i$.

Use an auxiliary $\mathbb P^{21}$ with one affine homogenizing coordinate, five coordinates squared to give the $Q_1$ coefficients, and sixteen cubed to give the cubic coefficients. On its affine chart this finite flat map has degree $2^5 3^{16}$, including at ramifications when local lengths are counted. Let $y$ denote its hyperplane class. Compactify the physical moving scalar in $\mathbb P^1$ with class $z$. The 25 norm equations assemble into a section of $W\otimes\mathcal O(6y)$. Indeed the physical residual is $t^6H-Q_1^3-A^2+B^2$. The two point conditions are sections of $\mathcal O(x+2z)$ and $\mathcal O(3y+3z)$: evaluating at $(a,b,c,d)=(\xi,r\xi,\alpha,s\alpha)$ makes them binary forms of degrees two and three in $(\xi,\alpha)$. All three bundles are globally generated on the product.

The direct sum has rank equal to the dimension of the smooth projective ambient space. General sections have zero-scheme length given by the top Chern class. Any isolated zero of this particular section contributes its local complete-intersection multiplicity to the limit under a generic perturbation. This bounds the sum of the isolated affine lengths even if there are excess components elsewhere. The top intersection is the $x^5y^{21}z$ coefficient of
$$
\left(\sum_{i=0}^{5}\binom{18+i}{i}x^i(6y)^{25-i}\right)(x+2z)(3y+3z).
$$
After dividing the finite flat cover degree, the upper bound is
$$
2^{16}3^5\left(3\binom{22}{4}+\binom{23}{5}\right)=885{,}348{,}237{,}312.
$$
This counts the distinguished affine cubic $h$ with its sign and both possible conjugacies. On the locus where the twelve-element action is free, the bound for the all-component quotient is $73{,}779{,}019{,}776$. The actual geometric degree can be smaller. The calculation includes excess solutions in the upper bound and does not establish a particular enumerative degree or a practical monodromy stopping criterion.
