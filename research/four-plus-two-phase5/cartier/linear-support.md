# The coupled target requires a nonlinear horizontal divisor

A rational primitive for the ratio-chart target $t^4$ cannot have all its finite fiber poles supported on the branch and a union of linear divisors in the four physical core coordinates. Arbitrary additional vertical poles are allowed in this assertion. In particular adding more planes to the support does not suffice.

Let $X=(H=0)\subset\mathbf P^3$. The ideal generated by $7H$ and the ten distinct $3\times3$ minors of its symmetric Hessian contains the full space of homogeneous polynomials of degree 23 in characteristic zero. Indeed the Macaulay matrices on the two sign parities each have size $2390\times1300$ and rank 1300 modulo 13, with maximal minors 2 and 5. The same ranks are obtained at 23 and 101. Thus the Hessian of $H$ has rank at least three at every point of $X$. At a smooth point $p$, Euler's identity pairs its radial direction with five times the gradient, so in a tangent-adapted basis the Hessian has rank two plus the rank of its quadratic form on the two projective tangent directions. The tangent plane therefore cuts $X$ to order exactly two. Every other plane passing through that point cuts transversely. [Integer generators and finite-field rank checks](plane_hessian.py)

Every plane section $B$ is reduced. A multiple curve in it would give a fiber of positive dimension for the Gauss map. The map pulls back the hyperplane class to the ample bundle $\mathcal O_X(5)$, which excludes a contracted curve. Resolve the triple cover of this plane branched along $B$. The dimensions of the two nontrivial eigenspaces of its irregularity, up to the convention for dual characters, are
$$h^1(\mathbf P^2,\mathcal J(kB/3)(2k-3)),\qquad k=1,2.$$
One can choose the auxiliary line at infinity transverse to the reduced branch. At multiplicity at most two the multiplier ideal $\mathcal J(B/3)$ is trivial. For instance at a double point Weierstrass preparation gives a unit times a monic quadratic in one local variable. Factoring that quadratic fiberwise, Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the integral of the product of the inverse $2/3$ powers of the two root distances by disk integrals of inverse $4/3$ powers, uniformly finite. The first eigenspace is consequently $H^1(\mathbf P^2,\mathcal O(-1))=0$. The invariant eigenspace also vanishes. [Naie, proof of Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0603427#page=11)

On the holomorphic one-forms only one nontrivial character can occur. Hence the weight-one rational Hodge structure splits into one-dimensional $\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$-summands of the same CM elliptic type. The Albanese of the resolution is isogenous to a power of the $j=0$ elliptic curve. Its Gysin image, with a Tate twist, has pure slope $3/2$ at the inert test primes. The same holds for the allowed vertical supports. The literal numerator has a nonzero slope-two component, proving the assertion. Descending the coefficients and using the good reduction of the target proceeds as in the vertical-support argument. For an irreducible reduced branch there is a separate elementary test which also applies to a smooth surface $S\subset\mathbf P^3$ of any degree. Such a surface is simply connected. For $U=S\setminus B$, localization gives finite cyclic $H_1(U,\mathbf Z)$: the group $H_2(S,U)$ is $\mathbf Z$ by duality with the top cohomology of the irreducible curve $B$. The image from $H_2(S)$ is nonzero by intersection with the hyperplane class. Let $G=\pi_1(U)$ and let $N$ correspond to the connected unbranched part of a cyclic cover of prime degree $p$. Then $N$ has finite index and is finitely generated. The finite group $G/([N,N]N^{p^k})$ is a $p$-group, and its space of homomorphisms to $\mathbf F_p$ has dimension at most one because it is a quotient of $G$. The Frattini basis theorem makes it cyclic. Its order is bounded by the order of $G^{\rm ab}$ for every $k$, which forces $N^{\rm ab}$ to have rank zero. The inclusion of the open cover into a smooth compactification surjects on fundamental groups by general position. Thus its resolution has zero first rational cohomology even when the irreducible branch has singularities. The two-character computation above also covers reducible plane sections.
