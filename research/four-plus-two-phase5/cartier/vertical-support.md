# What the slope test implies for the coupled current

For $A=a^3c^3$, there is no solution of the rank-three equation $\nabla_r B+\nabla_s C=-T$ over $\mathbf C(r,s)$. Equivalently any rational primitive for the original ratio-chart source needs a fiber pole away from $P=0$, $t=0$ and $t=\infty$. The argument combines the class-specific crystalline calculation with the geometry of vertical divisor surfaces. The moving-contact current with poles $t^2=\alpha^2$ remains eligible. [Rank-three reduction](../connection/gauss-manin.md) · [Class-specific slope test](target-cartier.md)

Suppose such a solution exists. Its lifted primitive in the function field of $T$ has poles only along the branch $w=0$, the complements of the chart $ac\ne0$, and surfaces over finitely many curves in the ratio base. The absolute de Rham class restricts to zero off those divisors, by the rational primitive and the residue formula on $w\ne0$. Equivalently, on this chart the residue of $A\Omega/(w^3+H)^2$ has density a nonzero constant times $t^4\,dr\,ds\,dt/w^5$. All coefficients may be taken algebraic over $\mathbf Q$: the proposed rational fields have some finite numerator and denominator degrees, and clearing denominators turns the differentiated identity into polynomial coefficient equations over $\mathbf Q$. Inverting one nonzero denominator coefficient preserves the witness on specialization to a closed point.

On a smooth projective threefold the kernel of restriction to the complement of a divisor is the sum of the images of $H^1(\widetilde D)(-1)$ for projective resolutions of its irreducible components. This follows from localization, duality and the fact that resolutions surject onto the pure weight-three part of the homology of the support. One can use equivariant resolutions for the cubic deck action $\rho$ and the sign $\sigma$. We project to the nontrivial cubic, sign-negative sector. The branch divisor has trivial cubic action. The surfaces $a=0$ and $c=0$ have $H^1=0$: each is a smooth cyclic triple cover of $\mathbf P^2$ with squarefree smooth sextic branch given by a binary sextic plus a sixth power. The three summands in the pushforward of its structure sheaf are $\mathcal O,\mathcal O(-2),\mathcal O(-4)$, all with zero $H^1$. Consider an irreducible base curve $C$ meeting the finite ratio chart. The generic fiber of its vertical surface is geometrically irreducible: a cubic power $P$ would require a triple root in $z$ or a degenerate endpoint, imposing two independent parameter conditions. More explicitly, at $b= krs\ne0$ the fixed ratio $[z^2]P/[z]P=2$ determines the scale of the roots. Any prescribed nonzero pair of ratios $f/g,b/g$ gives only finitely many base points, since $r=(b/g)g(s)/(ks)$ and substitution into $f(r)=(f/g)g(s)$ gives a degree-36 polynomial after clearing $s^6$. The same check at $f=0$ or $g=0$ uses the simple binary roots. A resolution $S$ of the vertical surface maps to the normalized base after resolving indeterminacy. The part of $H^1(S)$ pulled back from the base is invariant under both actions. The remaining part restricts to a constant abelian subquotient of the Jacobian of the normalized generic fiber. This is the fixed-part statement for a curve fibration, or follows by restricting $\mathrm{Pic}^0(S)$ to the fibers with connected generic fiber. The projected constant part is stable under $\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$.

When $bfg\Delta\ne0$ on the generic fiber, the cyclic cubic cover of the $t$-line has six simple branch points and genus four. Its sign-invariant quotient $w^3=-P(z)$ has genus one and $j=0$. The sign-negative Prym has dimension three, so its $H^1$ has rank three over $\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$. It varies on every such base curve. In fact, for a constant curve, the finite automorphism group lets one hold the cubic and sign actions fixed locally. An automorphism of the quotient $t$-line commuting with $t\mapsto-t$ is a scaling or a scaled inversion. The fixed middle-coefficient ratio permits only finitely many such scalings when comparing two branch sets. Isotriviality would then make $f/g,b/g$ constant, impossible by the finite-fiber computation. If the Prym had a constant $\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$-part of rank at least two, its polarization-orthogonal complement would have rank at most one. A rank-one polarized weight-one structure over this imaginary quadratic field has discrete period domain (its two complex Hodge lines have fixed characters). Together with the isotrivial quotient elliptic curve this would make the whole curve period map constant, contradicting Torelli. Hence every constant abelian factor in the projected sector has dimension at most one and is isogenous to the elliptic curve with CM by $\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)$.

The remaining base curves can be checked on the normalizations of their generic fibers.

* At $b=0$ the equation is diagonal. At $r=0$ use a simple zero of $g(s)$ and scale $t$ by $g^{-1/6}$, giving inertia characters $2j+1$ modulo six on $t^{2j}dt/w^2$ for $j=0,1,2$. At $s=0$ the corresponding exponents are $2j-3$. In either case the cube of the inertia generator sends $t$ to $-t$ in the smooth model and fixes the covering coordinate. Hence its sign-negative sector has no inertia invariants.
* At $f=0$ the branch set on the $t$-line has five points: a double zero at $0$ and four simple roots of $b+2bz+gz^2$. Its normalization has genus three. The sign-invariant quotient has genus one and $j=0$, so the projected Prym has dimension two. The configuration varies since $g(s)/b(s)$ is nonconstant, which changes the ratio of the two nonzero $z$ roots up to inversion. The ordinary genus and rank argument above bounds its constant CM part by one. At $g=0$ invert $t$ and exchange the binary roles.
* At $\Delta=0$ with $bfg\ne0$, the generic cubic is $g(z-u)^2(z-v)$ with $uv(u-v)\ne0$. Its cyclic cover in $t$ normalizes to genus two and its invariant quotient in $z$ has genus zero. The ratio $v/u$ varies, since otherwise the fixed middle-coefficient ratio would force the coefficient ratios to be constant. The same dimension-two bound applies. Triple roots occur only at finitely many base points. Fibers over individual base points in the chart have dimension one and supply no divisors.

Consequently each possible divisor contributes to this sector through $H^1$ of a CM elliptic curve, with the Gysin Tate twist. This factorization is algebraic: the projected Picard varieties and their isogenies realize the weight-one maps by correspondences, and all data descend to some number field. At an inert prime all such CM elliptic curves are potentially supersingular. By functoriality of $p$-adic comparison, the images in the good threefold have slope $3/2$, including after finite extension. The prescribed class has a slope-two component. This contradicts the proposed vertical support and proves the obstruction.
