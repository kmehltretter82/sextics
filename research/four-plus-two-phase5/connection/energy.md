# The energy direction on the coupled core

For `four-plus-two-residual`, varying the value of the affine core sextic gives a rank-12 connection. The prescribed source has no rational primitive using the energy direction alone in this connection, at any degree. Three exact two-direction tests give ranks 144 and 145 for the coefficient and augmented matrices at the numerator and denominator bounds below. A two-direction primitive with arbitrary base poles, or a primitive with moving poles on the plane fibers, remains possible.

## The plane fibers and the prescribed form

Use the chart $a=1$, with $r=b/a$ and $c,d$ the remaining affine coordinates. Set $k=5/7$ and

$$
 f(r)=1+kr+2kr^5+r^6,\qquad
 h=f(r)+kr(cd+2c^3d)+c^6+d^6+kc^5d+3kcd^5.
$$

Take $E=h$, $w^3=-E$ and write the target form as $c^3\,dr\wedge dc\wedge dd/w^5$, up to the fixed nonzero scalar of the residue convention. The source $a^3c^3$ restricts to $c^3$ on this chart. Work over $\mathbf Q(r,E)$ on the smooth affine curve $h=E$. For polynomial $P$ let $\eta(P)=-P\,dc/h_d=P\,dd/h_c$ as a relative differential. These expressions agree on overlaps and define a regular form on the fiber. At fixed $r$ they satisfy $dE\wedge\eta(P)=P\,dc\wedge dd$. An exact change $dK$ corresponds to $\{h,K\}=h_cK_d-h_dK_c$ in the numerator. Thus reduction modulo $(h-E)L+\{h,K\}$ computes regular relative differentials modulo exact differentials. The coefficient vectors below describe these classes, over a base field that allows rational functions of $r,E$.

The leading sextic has six simple roots at infinity. A smooth projective plane sextic has genus ten, so deleting the six points gives de Rham rank 25. For a homogeneous term of degree $n$, decompose its numerator into a representative for the leading Jacobian quotient and $g_c U+g_d V$, where $g$ is the binary leading sextic and $U,V$ have degree $n-5$. Euler's identity gives the following leading-degree lifts when $n\ge5$:

$$K=(dU-cV)/(n-4),\qquad L=6(U_c+V_d)/(n-4),\qquad
g_c U+g_d V=\{g,K\}+gL.$$

Subtracting $\{h,K\}+(h-E)L$ then lowers degree. The leading Jacobian ideal is generated by two coprime quintics. Its Hilbert series is $(1+z+z^2+z^3+z^4)^2$, so the reduction spans by 25 classes and the rank count gives independence. The sign involution $(c,d)\mapsto(-c,-d)$ fixes $\eta(1)$. We use the twelve odd basis monomials, ordered as in the code,

$$d,c,\quad d^3,cd^2,c^2d,c^3,\quad d^5,cd^4,c^2d^3,c^3d^2,\quad d^7,cd^6.$$

Write $e_A$ for the $c^3$ coordinate vector. The reduction and its polynomial lift are checked before each remainder is used. [Connection and reductions](energy_connection.py)

## Connection and the two-direction equation

Write $M_j$ for a basis monomial. First reduce $h M_j$ modulo the gradient ideal, giving a column $T_j$ and a polynomial vector field $V_j$ such that $hM_j=\sum_i (T_j)_iM_i+V_j(h)$. Apply the relative differential reduction above to $\operatorname{div} V_j$ and $h_rM_j$ to obtain columns $B_j$ and $C_j$. With $J=EI-T$, define the following matrices in the column convention:

$$\Gamma_E=(B-I)J^{-1},\qquad \Gamma_r=-\Gamma_E C-\partial_E C.$$

Indeed $\iota_{V_j}(dc\wedge dd)$ represents the relative form with column $J_j$. Differentiating in $E$ gives $\eta(\operatorname{div}V_j)$, hence $I+\Gamma_E J=B$. For the $r$ derivative at fixed $E$, the local coordinates $(r,E,c)$ give $\partial_r\eta(M_j)=-\partial_E\eta(h_rM_j)$. Differentiating the column $C_j$ gives the stated $\Gamma_r$. Multiplication by $w^{-5}$ adds $-5/(3E)$ in the energy direction. The two covariant derivatives are therefore

$$D_r=\partial_r+\Gamma_r,\qquad D_E=\partial_E+\Gamma_E-5/(3E).$$

For base components $X,Y$, use the relative current $w^{-5}(dE\wedge\eta(X)-dr\wedge\eta(Y))$. Its differentiated relative class is prescribed when

$$D_r X+D_E Y=e_A.$$

Every rational pair has a unique description $Y=CX+JZ$ on the generic open $\det J\ne0$. Since $\partial_E J=I$, direct substitution gives the equivalent equation

$$\partial_r X+C(\partial_E-5/(3E))X+J(\partial_E-5/(3E))Z+BZ=e_A.$$

This identity avoids inverting the critical-value polynomial in the bounded searches. The script checks both its sides and the curvature at the three regular points $(r,E)=(1,2),(2,-1),(-1,3)$.

## Critical values and residue ranks

The central critical point is $c=d=0$, at $E=f(r)$. A noncentral critical point has $cd\ne0$. Put $\theta=d/c$, $u=c^2$, $G=1+\theta^6+k\theta+3k\theta^5$, $A_0=3G-\theta G'$ and $b_0=2\theta G'-3G$. Differentiating $h=f+G u^3+kr\theta(u+2u^2)$ in $u,\theta$ gives

$$3G u^2+kr\theta(1+4u)=0,\qquad G'u^2+kr(1+2u)=0.$$

Eliminating $r$ gives $u=A_0/(2b_0)$ on the chart $\theta b_0\ne0$. The noncentral critical values there have parametrization

$$r=-A_0^2/(4k\theta b_0),\qquad
 E=f(r)+A_0^3(G-\theta G')/(8b_0^3).$$

At each of $r=1,2,-1$ the polynomial $A_0^2+4kr\theta b_0$ is squarefree of degree 12. All subsequent polynomial operations use the quotient of $\mathbf Q[\theta]$ by this degree-12 polynomial. The script checks the units in the displayed formulas, both critical equations, and the energy value in this algebra. Taking the resultant of the monic degree-12 polynomial with $E-h$ gives exactly $\det(EI-T)$. This polynomial has twelve distinct roots, separate from $f(r)$. In particular these specializations account for 24 distinct noncentral critical points in sign pairs. The central point does not contribute an eigenvalue of $T$ in the odd sector at these specializations.

## All rational pure-energy currents are excluded in this connection

Set $X=0$ and first take $r=1$. Write $T_1,B_1$ for the specialization. Both are constant matrices. The characteristic polynomial of $T_1$ has simple roots, avoids zero, and the exact traces $\operatorname{tr}((B_1-I)T_1^j)$ vanish for $0\le j<12$. For any eigenvalue $u$ of $T_1$, its spectral projector $P_u$ is a polynomial in $T_1$ of degree at most 11. The residue $(B_1-I)P_u$ of $\Gamma_E$ therefore has rank at most one and trace zero, hence is nilpotent. A pole of order $m>0$ in $Y$ would require the residue to have eigenvalue $m$ by comparing the leading coefficients of $D_E Y=e_A$. This rules out poles at all twelve critical values and at all ordinary finite points. The residue at $E=0$ equals $-5I/3$, so $Y$ is also regular there and its constant term must vanish.

Consequently $Y$ is polynomial in $E$. At infinity $\Gamma_E=(B_1-I)/E+O(E^{-2})$. If its degree were $n>1$, cancellation of the coefficient of $E^{n-1}$ would require $8/3-n$ to be an eigenvalue of $B_1$. The computed eigenvalues with multiplicities are $1/2$ (2), $5/6$ (4), $7/6$ (4), $3/2$ (2). None gives such an integer $n$. The only remaining candidate is $Y=-3Ee_A/2$, forced by the constant coefficient of the equation at zero. Substitution at $E=2$ gives the nonzero residual $-3\Gamma_E(2)e_A$.

Finally the normalization $r=1$ cannot discard a rational solution over $\mathbf C(r,E)$. All entries of $\Gamma_E$ are regular along the generic point of $r=1$. If $Y$ had a pole there, its leading Laurent coefficient in $r-1$ would be a nonzero rational horizontal vector at $r=1$. At $E=0$ the leading exponent of a horizontal vector would have to equal $5/3$, whereas a rational vector has integral valuation. Thus a hypothetical solution would specialize to the equation just excluded. This argument holds over every characteristic-zero constant extension of $\mathbf Q$.

## The bounded searches with both directions

For each of the three denominators $d_0$ below, take $X=N_X/d_0$ and $Z=N_Z/d_0$. Each of the 24 polynomial components of $N_X,N_Z$ has separate degree bounds $\deg_r\le2$ and $\deg_E\le1$. Multiplying the pole-cleared equation by $3Ed_0^2$ gives polynomial coefficient equations over $\mathbf Q$. Their ranks are computed by exact row reduction.

| $d_0$ | Equations | Unknowns | Coefficient rank | Augmented rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $1$ | 286 | 144 | 144 | 145 |
| $E-r^3$ | 438 | 144 | 144 | 145 |
| $E-f(r)$ | 556 | 144 | 144 | 145 |

The degree bounds apply to $X,Z$ in the stated coordinates. The rank inequalities leave other numerator degrees, other pole divisors, and currents with additional relative poles open. They do not determine the Phase 4 normalized trace or its rational correction. [Exact tests](energy_tests.py)

Run these commands from this directory with Python 3 and SymPy. Installing python-flint as a SymPy backend speeds up the exact row reductions.

```sh
python3 energy_connection.py
python3 energy_tests.py
```
