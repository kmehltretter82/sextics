# Low-degree elliptic maps on possible base supports

For a moving curve in the ratio base, consider a compatible map from its smooth generic fiber $w^3=-P(t)$ to a constant elliptic curve over the function field of that curve. The sector under consideration has cubic-deck character $\zeta_3$ and sign character $-1$ under $t\mapsto-t$. We classify equivariant maps to $E:Y^3=X^2+1$ through degree five and show that the corresponding base loci carry no rational horizontal section. The conclusion concerns base residues. It leaves open moving-in-$t$ poles in the original three-variable field.

The elliptic actions are $Y\mapsto\zeta_3Y$ and $X\mapsto-X$. Equivariance gives $X=A(t)/B(t)$ odd and $Y=M(t)w$. The source-to-target degree is the degree $d$ of $A/B$ because each quotient by the cubic action has degree three. Over an algebraic closure of the base field choose coprime homogeneous $A,B$ of degree $d$. The relation $1+(A/B)^2=-PM^3$ partitions the six branch points of $P$. Absorbing scalar factors, write $P=P_1P_2$, $B=P_2L_0^3$, $A^2+B^2=P_1N^3$. In fact each branch that is a pole of $X$ has pole order $1$ modulo $3$, and its opposite point is again a pole. At remaining poles the orders are multiples of three. The same valuation comparison applies to $A^2+B^2$. Thus $\deg P_2$ is even and congruent to $d$ modulo three. Both $A$ and $B$ have definite and opposite parity by coprimality. The smooth generic case has $fg\ne0$, so $0,\infty$ are outside the six branch points. Degree one is impossible by genus.

* For $d=2$ one has $\deg P_2=2$. In the affine chart $B$ is even quadratic and $A$ is linear odd. Up to constant $P=B(B^2+A^2)$. If $b\ne0$, the ratio of the $z^2$ and $z$ coefficients forces the ratio of the $z$ and constant coefficients of $B$ to be $2$. Comparing the top and constant terms of $P$ now gives $g=8f$. The rank-three residue there vanishes. [Proportional pencil calculation](gauss-manin.md)
* For $d=3$ one has $\deg P_2=0$. The homogeneous $L_0$ is $t$ or the homogenizing variable up to scalar, by parity. In the latter case $B$ is constant and $P/f=1+(a_1t+a_3t^3)^2$. Coefficient comparison gives $g=b$ when $b\ne0$. In the reversed chart it gives $f=b/8$. The first example maps by $(t,w)\mapsto(t+t^3,w)$ into $Y^3=-f-bX^2$. Every member $g=c b$, $c\ne0$, has zero rational horizontal space. On its rational normalization $r=g(s)/(ck s)$ take a simple zero of $g$. There $f=1$, and after adjoining $\xi=g^{1/6}$ and setting $t=\tau/\xi$, the limiting fiber is $w^3=-1-\tau^6$. The inertia action on $\tau^{2j}d\tau/w^2$ has odd characters $2j+1$ modulo six for $j=0,1,2$. No invariant vector occurs. A rational flat section would specialize to an invariant vector in the smooth model, where regular horizontal sections are determined by their value. The same argument for $f=c b$ uses $\xi=f^{1/6}$, $t=\xi\tau,w=\xi^2w'$ with odd characters $2j-3$. It also disposes of the coordinate branches $r=0$ and $s=0$ where $b=0$.
* For $d=4$ one has $\deg P_2=4$ and $B=P_2$ is even. Normalize $B\pm iA=(t\mp u)(t\mp v)^3$, since the two factors are coprime, monic, interchanged by sign and each contains one of the opposite $P_1$ roots. Put $h=u/v$, $L=v^2$. Then $P=gL^3Q_h(t/v)$, where

$$Q_h(\tau)=(\tau^2-h^2)(\tau^4+3(1+h)\tau^2+h).$$

* For $d=5$ one has $\deg P_2=2$, and inversion interchanges the two possibilities for the linear $L_0$. Choose the chart where $B=P_2$ is even quadratic and $A$ is odd quintic. The conjugate factors of $A^2+B^2$ have one triple root apiece. Normalize $A+iB=(t-v)^3(t^2+3vt+hv^2)$; the lack of a quartic term forces the displayed linear coefficient. Again $P=gL^3Q_h(t/v)$ with $L=v^2$, now with

$$Q_h(\tau)=(\tau^4+(2h-9)\tau^2+h^2)((8-3h)\tau^2-h)/(8-3h).$$

Write $Q_h=\tau^6+V\tau^4+U\tau^2+W$. Matching the two middle terms of the protected sextic gives $L=V/(2U)$, $b/g=L^2 U$, $f/g=L^3 W$. After inversion the constant $2$ changes to $1/2$ and the two binary polynomials exchange roles, which preserves the valuations below. For degrees four and five, respectively, choose a root $h_0$ of $1-3h-3h^2$ or take $h_0=-1$. At these points $U$ vanishes simply while $V,W,\operatorname{disc}_\tau Q_h$ are units, as verified exactly. Constant $h$ amounts to fixed ratios $f/g,b/g$. Their nonzero finite levels are zero-dimensional, by eliminating $r=(b/g)g/(ks)$ and observing the degree-36 numerator in $s$.

On the incidence cover adjoining $h$, let $\epsilon=h-h_0$. Then $b/g=m/\epsilon$, $f/g=n/\epsilon^3$ for analytic units $m,n$. At each of the six simple roots $s_0$ of $g$ use the local variable $y=g(s)$ and substitute $r=my/(ks\epsilon)$ into $f(r)=ny/\epsilon^3$. Clearing denominators gives monomials with $(\operatorname{ord}_y,\operatorname{ord}_\epsilon)$ starting at $(0,6),(1,5),(5,1),(6,0),(1,3)$. The lower edges have binomial initial forms with unit coefficients and horizontal lengths one and five. They give one root with $\operatorname{ord}_\epsilon y=3$ and five conjugate roots with order $3/5$, all simple after the indicated base changes. These account for all $6(1+5)=36$ roots of the degree-36 equation in $s$, so every irreducible component has such a point. There $g$ has order three in a uniformizer of the normalized component and $L$ has odd pole order (one or five). The normalized fiber $Q_h$ is smooth. In $t=\sqrt L\,\tau$, $w=L g^{1/3} w'$, the cube root exists locally while the square root changes sign around the point. Inertia acts by $-1$ on all $\tau^{2j}d\tau/w'^2$. Consequently no rational flat vector exists even on this incidence cover, and none descends to the original ratio curve. [Symbolic checks](elliptic_fibers.py)
