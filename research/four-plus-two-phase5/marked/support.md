# One support polynomial suffices for the literal primitive

Let $k=\mathbf Q$, $r=b/a$, $s=d/c$, $t=c/a$, $w^3=-P$, with $P=f_X(r)+(5/7)rs(t^2+2t^4)+f_Y(s)t^6$. The prescribed form is $t^4\,dr\wedge ds\wedge dt/w^5$. Use the already established compact pullback-rank result for the characteristic-zero contact component through the smooth 11-adic seed. Its pullback on the 48-dimensional anti-invariant sector of $H^{2,1}$ is injective for the coordinate involution $(c,d)\mapsto(-c,-d)$. Complex conjugation, the absence of $H^{3,0}$ in that sign, and duality make its proper cylinder pushforward surjective on $H^3_-$. Equivalently, the projected proper cycles use the difference of the contact and its sign translate. Therefore every such compact class restricts to zero off the swept surface and its sign translate. Finite deck translates may also be removed. In particular it suffices to compute **any nonzero rational polynomial containing their projected images**, without computing the cylinder current, its covering degree, a scalar, or a linear combination of its inputs.

## A rational support given by a resultant

Use the 24 square marked equations $F_i(y)$ of [the marked Segre chart](README.md), with signed $h$, working over $K=k(r,s)$. No inverse equations are needed for this construction. At $r=4,s=2$ their Jacobian determinant is 8 modulo 11, so the characteristic-zero marked branch over $K$ consists generically of simple roots. Let $d_i=\deg_y F_i$, let $F_i^h$ be its ordinary homogenization, and choose an ordering of the 24 affine coordinates $y_i$; denote their homogeneous coordinates by $Y_i$, and the homogenizing one by $Y_0$. If $Y_j$ corresponds to $Z=(c/a)^2$, form

$$R(\epsilon,u)=\operatorname{Res}_{Y_0,\ldots,Y_{24}}(F_1^h+\epsilon Y_1^{d_1},\ldots,F_{24}^h+\epsilon Y_{24}^{d_{24}},Y_j-uY_0).$$

This is a nonzero polynomial over $K$. Indeed the top-degree forms at $Y_0=0$ have nonzero resultant over $K(\epsilon)$: its leading $\epsilon$ coefficient is the resultant of the pure powers. The perturbed intersection is therefore finite (a positive-dimensional projective component would meet $Y_0=0$). Its coordinates do not exhaust the independent variable $u$. Write $\Psi(r,s,u)$ for the first nonzero coefficient of $R$ in $\epsilon$, clearing $K$-denominators. At each chosen simple root $y_*$ over $\overline K$ the implicit-function theorem gives an integral series $y(\epsilon)$ satisfying the perturbed equations and specializing to $y_*$. Substituting $u=y_j(\epsilon)$ in $R$, dividing by the lowest power of $\epsilon$ and specializing gives $\Psi(r,s,y_{*,j})=0$. This argument only requires the chosen root to be simple. Positive-dimensional components, roots at infinity, and extraneous factors of the answer are all permitted. The resulting nonzero $\Psi(r,s,t^2)$ vanishes identically on the chosen swept component, all constant-field conjugates and its sign/deck translates. This is the generalized characteristic-polynomial perturbation. [Canny, *Generalized Characteristic Polynomials*](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1988/6040.html)

A coupled test uses the physical $\mathbf F_{11}$ quadric and cubic of the smooth seed. Fix $a=1,b=4$, allowing $c=t,d$ to vary. Their exact resultant in $d$ is $f=t^6+4t^5-4t^4+3t^3+2t^2-4t-1$; the marked physical point is $(t,d)=(6,1)$, where the two-variable Jacobian is 3. Multiplying both equations by $d$ introduces an entire unwanted line and makes the ordinary resultant zero. Perturb the resulting cubics/quartics by $\epsilon t^3,\epsilon d^4$. Its first nonzero coefficient is at order one and equals $f(-4t^6+t^5+t^4+t^3)$. The checker verifies the actual sextic norm relation modulo the quadric before testing the perturbation. This tests the support mechanism on one known contact curve; the 24-equation surface resultant has not been evaluated. [Exact checker](support_projection.py)

## A one-index linear primitive calculation

Set $M=tP\Psi(r,s,t^2)$ and $B=k[r,s,t,1/M]$. On the affine cyclic cover $U=\operatorname{Spec}B[w]/(w^3+P)$ the proper support has been removed. The ratio chart also requires $a,c,w\ne0$, as imposed here. The target is the restriction of its compact residue class, up to the nonzero normalization convention. Hence it is exact on $U$ by the compact support argument and affine algebraic de Rham. Vanishing after extension to $\mathbf C$ implies vanishing over $\mathbf Q$ by flat base change of the global differential complex. Since $w$ is a unit, $\Omega_U^1$ is free over $\mathcal O(U)$ on $dr,ds,dt$, and the coefficient basis $1,w,w^2$ splits the differential into its three cubic-degree components over $B$. The component of an arbitrary primitive that differentiates to the target has the form $w^{-2}\iota_q(dr\wedge ds\wedge dt)$ with $q_i\in B$.

For $m=0,1,\ldots$ seek polynomials $N_i$ of degree $\le m(1+\deg M)$ satisfying

$$\sum_{i\in\{r,s,t\}} (2M P_i+3m P M_i)N_i-3PM\sum_i\partial_iN_i=3t^4M^{m+1}.$$

These are linear systems over $\mathbf Q$. The first successful one gives exactly $q_i=N_i/M^m$, verified by polynomial expansion. This one-index search *provably terminates* on this support: any primitive in $B$ has a finite numerator degree and some common power $M^{m_0}$; increasing $m$ raises its numerator degree by only $(m-m_0)\deg M$. The target and all signs follow from $w^{-5}=-w^{-2}/P$. Neither the first successful $m$ nor its numerators have yet been computed.

The seven-variable marked reduction gives another input to the same construction. Form its rank-480 multiplication determinant and six directional derivatives over the base, and clear their rational denominators. On the verified open the seven functions generate the full marked scheme locally, with the same simple root as the original 24 equations. Applying the displayed perturbation to these seven equations, with $Z$ as one of the seven coordinates, therefore gives a support polynomial as well. The two sizes involve different coefficient degrees and no comparison of running time follows. [Seven-variable algebra and its finite-fiber check](README.md#seven-equations-and-a-complete-two-parameter-slice)

## An alternative to computing the whole support resultant

Work over the number field of the chosen isolated algebraic seed or its algebraic closure. Let $S\subset\mathbf P^3$ be its irreducible projected swept image of degree $\delta$. A generic projective line meets it in $\delta$ distinct generic physical points and every point has at least one isolated lift to the contact system with the marked point on that line. The multidegrees and vector bundles in the previously proved [isolated-solution bound](https://github.com/kmehltretter82/sextics/blob/97fbaf87b5e238d50935c8474a3e1db2f212a2e0/research/four-plus-two-phase5/quadric/finite_chart.md) depend only on the point being parametrized linearly on a $\mathbf P^1$. The quadratic and cubic point constraints have degrees two and three on any line. Consequently the same bound $B_0=885{,}348{,}237{,}312$ applies to $\delta$, independent of the actual moving degree. It is a worst-case stopping bound, many orders above current computations.

Choose a generic plane through the known physical point. Intersect it with the local two-dimensional implicit-function branch of the incidence. For a homogeneous degree-$n$ polynomial $D$ on $\mathbf P^3$, suppose $D$ restricts to order strictly greater than $n\delta$ on that plane arc. Then $D$ vanishes on $S$: otherwise a generic plane cuts no common curve of $D$ and $S$ and Bézout bounds their total local intersection lengths on the plane by $n\delta$. This applies to a single local smooth branch even when another analytic branch has the same projected point. The plane must be generic over the coefficient field of $D$; when working with symbolic slopes, require the unknown coefficients of $D$ to be independent of them. Thus the surface equation can be recovered from the jets of the *one* chosen implicit-function branch by finite linear algebra for $n=1,\ldots,B_0$. At $n=\delta$ the exact-degree kernel is its line of defining equations. Taking a number-field norm and multiplying by the sign translate gives rational support for the primitive calculation above.

A shorter a posteriori certification can accompany practical jet fitting. Let $I=(F_i)$ in the original incidence coordinates (include chart inverses), now with $r,s$ variable and either $\alpha^2=Z$ adjoined or a sign-even polynomial $D$ used. The localized incidence ring at the selected point $p$ is regular by the unit Jacobian and therefore a domain. The candidate vanishes on the chosen component iff there is $g$ with $g(p)=1$ and $gD\in I$. For fixed $D$ and degree bounds, $gD=\sum H_iF_i$ and $g(p)=1$ are linear conditions on $g,H_i$. Dovetail this search with prolonging Hensel jets; true membership yields a finite syzygy and false membership a nonzero Taylor coefficient. One can also simply verify the final rational primitive directly before seeking any geometric support certificate.

To check the physical parameters, the actual marked system modulo 121 gives $(Z_r,Z_s)=(9,1)$, $(t_r,t_s)=(9,1)$, and $(d_r,d_s)=(7,8)$ modulo 11. With $a=1$ its projected tangent plane is $10b+3c+d=4$. In the generic plane $d-d_0=m(b-b_0)+n(c-c_0)$ the $s$ derivative of the defining equation is $8-n$, a unit at generic slope. Thus $b-b_0$ is an actual uniformizer along this plane arc and the required jets are obtained by a square implicit solve. [Tangent checker](support_tangent.py)
