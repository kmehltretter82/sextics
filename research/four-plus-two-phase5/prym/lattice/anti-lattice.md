# A norm −12 test for contact quadrics

Work geometrically in characteristic zero. Let Q be a smooth block-even quadric, with its two rulings preserved by the involution σ. Write C for the smooth section cut out by the actual sextic H and W for the cyclic cover u³=−H of Q. Put J=π*O(1,1). On the nonempty Zariski open established below there is an equivalence

> H|Q = q³ + c h·σh if and only if Pic(W) contains a class v with σv = −v and v² = −12.
Here q is an even (2,2) section and h is a (3,3) section. Constants or either sign can be absorbed over the algebraic closure. The assertion permits reducible h. Smoothness of the moving genus-four curve and disjoint contact divisors are separate open conditions, verified on the seeded branch in the previous packet. The equivalence detects the support in the five-dimensional space of quadrics. It gives no degree or multiplicity of the support and no rational flux certificate.

## Graphs and short vectors

The cyclic cover formula gives J²=6, K_W=2J and the direct image of O_W equal to O, O(−2,−2), O(−4,−4) in direct sum. Thus χ(O_W)=11 and q(W)=0. For torsion-freeness of Pic(W), the ordinary complete intersection Q=0, e⁶+H=0 in P⁴ is smooth (use the e-derivative away from e=0 and smoothness of C at e=0) and simply connected by Lefschetz. W is its quotient by e↦−e, which has fixed points. A loop based at an unbranched point may be moved off the branch and lifted to a path. If the lifted endpoints differ, append a path from the endpoint to a fixed point and the reflected reverse path back to the first endpoint; their projections cancel. Thus every loop projects from a closed loop. The universal-coefficient sequence and q(W)=0 then give torsion-freeness.

Given a contact, its graph divisor D=(u+q,h) maps isomorphically to (h=0) in Q and satisfies D+σD∼2J. Thus v=D−J is integral and anti-invariant. Adjunction on Q gives arithmetic genus four, so adjunction on W gives D²=−6 and v²=−12. The cubic deck ρ has 1+ρ+ρ²=π*π_* on divisor classes. This kills v since σ acts trivially on Pic(Q). The two vectors v,ρv have Gram matrix with diagonal −12 and off-diagonal 6.

Conversely, for any anti-invariant line class v, J.v=0 and

> χ(J+v) = 8 + v²/2 and h²(J+v) = h⁰(J+v).
The latter is Serre duality followed by σ. In particular v²=−12 supplies a nonzero section. For D its zero divisor, D+σD has section sσs in H⁰(W,2J)^σ = ⟨u⟩⊕H⁰(Q,O(2,2))^σ. The symmetric norm linearization agrees with the ordinary one, as may be tested at any fixed point of W over a fixed point of Q. If its u-coordinate is nonzero, then the norm divisor is the graph of −q over (H−q³=0). Its closed subscheme D is an effective pure curve on smooth Q, hence a divisor there. Its two ruling degrees are each 3 since σv=−v. It therefore has a (3,3) equation h and the norm identity follows. This also shows that a divisor admitting a graph norm always has v²=−12, whatever its initial norm.

There are no integral anti-invariant vectors of norm −2, −4 or −6 on any W as above. Indeed Riemann–Roch gives h⁰(J+v)≥3. Every section norm has u-coordinate zero. For any independent s₀,s₁, polarize the norms of s₀, s₁ and s₀+s₁. The rational functions fσf and f+σf for f=s₁/s₀ both lie in k(Q). The field extension k(W)/k(Q) has degree 3. Since f satisfies a quadratic over k(Q), f descends. Consequently the complete linear system on W has a fixed part plus the pullback of a moving system on Q. Clear the common pole divisor of the ratios downstairs and remove the downstairs fixed divisors. Finite pullback preserves the absence of fixed divisors. The moving class (a,b) satisfies 3(a,b)≤(3,3) by positivity and pushing forward. At least three independent sections force (a,b)=(1,1). The remaining fixed part then has zero pushforward and vanishes, giving v=0. Since the triangular lattice generated by v,ρv has minimum squared length 12 and squared covering radius 4, this also proves that its inclusion in Pic(W) is primitive. Indeed any putative new class in its rational span gives a nonzero integral anti-invariant class of squared length at most 4 after subtracting a nearest lattice vector.

## The geometric exceptions

If the coefficient of u vanishes then D+σD=π*(q) for an even (2,2) divisor q. Over any irreducible component B, the pullback is either irreducible or splits into three components, since the irreducible branch curve C cannot occur in q. When B is invariant, σ fixes the pullback if irreducible, and fixes each of the three components in the split case because the centralizer of the transitive 3-cycle on those components has odd order. Such components contribute zero to D−σD. For a noninvariant pair B,σB with irreducible preimages their contribution to D−σD is a multiple of π*(B−σB), zero in Pic(W). Since D−σD=2v is nonzero, some noninvariant irreducible B must split, i.e. H|B must be a cube in its function field upon trivializing the cubic line bundle. The sum B+σB lies in (2,2). Thus B is a ruling or smooth (1,1) conic. A rational cube of a regular section on either of these smooth curves has a regular cube root by valuations. In particular, exclusion of these two low-degree curve types makes the norm −12 test sufficient for every class.

## Uniqueness and extension of the section

On the open excluding split rulings and conics, each nonzero integral anti-invariant v with v²>−16 has a section of J+v by Riemann–Roch. The preceding component argument forces its norm to have nonzero u-coordinate. Adjunction then forces v²=−12. Thus all other negative even squares strictly above −16 are excluded here.

For v²=−12, the entire divisor D embeds as a closed subscheme of the graph (u+q=0), which projects isomorphically to its image on Q by elimination of u. Thus projection is a closed immersion on D and on every constituent, regardless of invariance. At the generic point of every constituent B one has H=q³, so no constituent can be a ruling or (1,1) curve on this open. An irreducible effective curve with one ruling degree zero must be a single ruling over the algebraic closure. Every constituent therefore has both degrees positive and sum of degrees at least three. Counting all multiplicities in total bidegree (3,3), there is either one irreducible reduced (3,3) curve, or there are two constituents with degrees (1,2) and (2,1). The latter must be distinct with multiplicity one since twice a bidegree cannot equal (3,3). In particular the argument includes invariant constituents without requiring a separate assumption about them.

In the irreducible case D²=−6 forces every effective representative of its class to contain D. In the second case the two constituents E and E′ are smooth rational curves. Indeed a smooth quadric gives arithmetic genus zero for a (1,2) or (2,1) divisor, so an integral such divisor is smooth of genus zero. Adjunction on smooth W gives E²=(E′)²=−8. The equality D²=−6 gives E.E′=5 and D.E=D.E′=−3. Every effective representative must therefore contain both. After these components are subtracted, the residual effective divisor has intersection zero with the ample class J, and vanishes. This proves h⁰(J+v)=1. By duality h²(J+v)=1 and Riemann–Roch gives h¹(J+v)=0. It follows that the marked graph divisors over each good Q are individually indexed by its anti-invariant norm −12 line classes, a finite set by the Hodge index theorem.

One infinitesimal consequence is unconditional here. Given a proper smooth deformation of W through such a point over a local Artin base, and a lift of the line bundle J+v, its unique projective section lifts uniquely. For a small extension with square-zero ideal I annihilated by the maximal ideal, tensor the restriction exact sequence of the lifted line bundle with the central fiber. The obstruction to lifting a section belongs to H¹(W,J+v)⊗I=0, and the ambiguity consists of H⁰(W,J+v)⊗I, exactly scalar multiples of the chosen section. Induction in the length of the base proves the assertion. This describes the marking over the relative Picard functor. A global identification with a period zero scheme, as well as any resulting modular or enumerative formula, would require further arguments.

## The actual quadric

Use the F₁₁ Segre seed in ../prym_seed.py with (m,n,u,v,e)=(2,6,1,7,7). The projective exclusion described below applies over the whole algebraic closure. It avoids an exhaustive enumeration of conics over a field of bounded degree.

* For a ruling write F(z)=H(w,z). The numerator of the third derivative of F^(1/3) must vanish identically on every ruling carrying a cube. For both rulings and both parameter charts the gcd of its coefficient polynomials is 1.
* Every smooth (1,1) conic is the graph of a projectivity. If H restricted to it is a cube, every intersection with the branch has multiplicity at least three. At a smooth intersection, in finite affine coordinates the gradient entries H_w,H_z must both be nonzero. The unique osculating graph there is z+m t/(1−kt), with t the difference in w, m=−H_w/H_z and k=(H_ww H_z²−2H_wz H_w H_z+H_zz H_w²)/(2H_w H_z²). Its cleared restriction is t³G(t). A necessary condition for a cube is that the fourth derivative of G^(1/3) vanish. Compute its numerator by V₀=1, V_{j+1}=3G V'_j+(1−3j)G'V_j. The first three coefficient resultants against H in the z-coordinate give candidate w-degrees 1795, 101, 101 after radical and intersections. All content canceled during the symbolic calculation and the leading z-coefficient are reinserted. The final candidate still has degree 101. Each entire fiber is then retested over the residue fields by direct expansion and all coefficients of V₄, including coordinate-infinite fibers and both z charts (the z=∞ candidates have leading z-coefficient zero, which was included). All 898 noncritical geometric points counted with chart overlap are excluded.
* The homogeneous gradient Macaulay matrix in bidegree (19,19) has 400 columns and rank 395. Five distinct singularities are the corner w=∞,z=0 and the four roots of z⁴+1=0, w=(z³+2z)/5. Six distinct projective points already give six independent evaluations at this bidegree (multiply separating linear forms in the Segre embedding), so these are all the geometric singularities. Each is an ordinary node by its Hessian. A conic meeting C solely in nodes must either have multiplicity at least six at one node or pass through four nodes with multiplicity three at each. In the first case its tangent matches a branch and the transverse branch contributes one, so its second-order jet is the unique osculating graph of that branch. In the second case any three of the four nodes determine the smooth conic. The ten branch osculators and ten three-point conics were tested over F_{11⁴} by exact univariate factorization including infinity. None has a cubic restriction. The same arguments exclude an identically vanishing restriction (it would pass through a smooth intersection also).

The condition of a cube restriction is closed here even under degeneration of a smooth conic. Over the dual P³ of hyperplane sections of the fixed quadric, the spaces H⁰(E,O_E(2,2)) and H⁰(E,O_E(6,6)) form bundles by the restriction sequence. Their fibers have dimension 5 and 13, respectively. Every E is a smooth conic or a reduced pair of rulings. Thus cubing gives a morphism of their projective bundles with closed image. There is no zero restriction H|E in the special fiber by the checks above. Any limit at a ruling pair would give a cubic restriction on each ruling. The same incidence works relatively in the space of smooth block-even quadrics, with the two ruling families treated after an étale base change. Properness transfers the geometric exclusion to the known characteristic-zero lift and gives a Zariski open through that point. On this open the smooth-branch condition is imposed in characteristic zero as proved by the earlier 11-adic gradient calculation.
